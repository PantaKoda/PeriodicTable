# Choose the source and data strategy

Type: research
Status: resolved
Blocked by:

## Question

Which high-trust primary sources or first-party APIs can support natural occurrence, native occurrence, biological role, and common uses across all 118 elements, under terms that permit bundling concise derived descriptions and visible citations in this project?

The answer must compare coverage, authority, access method, stability, licensing/attribution expectations, and gaps—especially synthetic elements—and recommend a source hierarchy rather than a single-source shortcut.

## Comments

## Answer

Use a hand-curated offline dataset with field-level citations and review dates; production must not depend on a runtime API. Use IUPAC/PubChem for identity and provenance, USGS for occurrence and commodity uses, NIH ODS plus CDC/ATSDR for biological context, and DOE or first-party discovery laboratories for radioactive and superheavy elements. Prefer reusable government material, write original summaries, preserve isotope/compound qualifiers and uncertainty, and treat RSC/LANL/JLab or restrictively licensed IUPAC prose as cited corroboration rather than copy or translation sources.

The complete evidence, licensing analysis, source hierarchy, and synthetic-element edge cases are recorded in [Source and data strategy](../research/source-and-data-strategy.md).
